# Testing: Stage Manager

**Audience:** us.

Scope: the panel, drag to move, the stored map and the re-apply on load, statuses, the lobby scene and the chat button (`scripts/manager-stage.js`, `scripts/window-stage.js`). The state rules are covered by the Stage Manager suite in the test harness; everything here needs a GM client and at least one player client on a second browser or profile. Remove an item when it passes, and delete this file when it is empty. Results go to the **Verified** line of the CHANGELOG entry, not back into this file.

1. Open it. As the GM click the Stage Manager menubar icon (the masks). Expected: a window titled Stage Manager with a card for the active scene, badged Active, and one for the scene you are viewing if different; every connected player appears as a chip on the scene they are looking at; GMs have a crown.
2. Live location. A player opens a different scene from their own scene navigation (if they can) or you pull them with core's tools. Expected: within a moment their chip is on the other card, without reloading the panel.
3. Drag to move. Drag a player's chip onto another scene's card. Expected: the card highlights while the chip is over it, the player's view changes to that scene, and the chip now sits on that card with a pin icon (held).
4. A hold survives a reload. With a player held on a scene that is not active, have them reload (F5). Expected: they load into the held scene, not the active one.
5. A hold survives you being away. Close the GM client, have the held player reload. Expected: still the held scene; no GM is needed.
6. Release. Click the x on a held chip. Expected: the pin goes; the player stays where they are for now; after they reload they land on the active scene.
7. Moving to the active scene. Drag a held player onto the active scene's card. Expected: they move there and are no longer marked held.
8. Offline player. Disconnect a held player. Expected: their chip stays on the held scene, dashed and dimmed; reconnect and they load into it.
9. Add a stage. Drag a scene from the Scenes sidebar onto the Stage Manager, onto a card and onto empty space. Expected: it gets a card ("Nobody here. Drop a player to send them."); drop a player on it to send them; the x on an empty hand-added card takes it off.
10. Return Everyone. With two players held on two scenes, press Return Everyone and confirm. Expected: every hold is cleared and every connected player is pulled to the active scene.
11. Core pull is not undone. Pull a held player to another scene with Foundry's own Pull to Scene. Expected: they go there and are not yanked back until they reload.
12. Combat badge. Start a combat on a stage. Expected: its card shows a badge with the count; end it and the badge goes.
13. GM only. As a player, the menubar icon is absent; `StageWindow.open()` from the console warns and does nothing.
14. Active scene change. Activate a different scene while players are held. Expected: players move as Foundry normally does; the holds stay listed; held players return to their held scene on their next reload.
15. Activate. Press the bullseye on a scene that is not active. Expected: it becomes the active scene (Active badge moves, the bullseye on it is lit and disabled), and players follow as they do from the scene navigation. The eye is separate and only moves your own view. Double-click the empty part of a card, or its title: same result. Double-click a person, a button or a token: nothing activates.
16. Portraits. Expected: each player is a square portrait with no name on it (their character's, else their avatar), a GM or someone with neither shows an icon in the same square, hovering shows the name, and a held player also shows a small pin.
17a. Drop zone only when dropping. At rest there is no dashed box, wash or extra padding, and the player portraits are large (about 36px). Start dragging a chip (or a character from the sidebar): every card's drop zone appears with a dashed edge, goes solid orange under the pointer, and the empty stages show their hint; let go or cancel the drag and it all disappears without anything jumping.
17. Scene backgrounds. Expected: each card is three parts: the title row (name, badges, buttons), the content area below it, and the drop zone inside that. The scene's thumbnail is the background of the whole card, behind the title row and the content area, under a light wash in the window theme's own colour, with a visible margin of picture around the drop zone and the title row readable over it. The dashed drop zone inside is nearly transparent, so the picture shows through it. People are solid themed pills. Check on Light, Glass and dark: pale wash and dark text on Light, dark wash and light text on the others. A scene with no thumbnail shows the plain themed content area. The thumbnail is the small generated one, so it is soft.
18. Drag from the sidebar. Drag a player's character from the Actors directory onto a card. Expected: the player who has that character assigned (else owns it) is moved there; dragging an NPC that no player owns warns and moves nobody; dragging a token from the canvas does the same as its actor. Dragging a user from Foundry's player list moves that user.
19. Drag yourself. Drag the GM's own chip onto another card. Expected: your view moves to that scene, nothing is held or stored, and no player moves. Another GM's chip, if there is one, cannot be dragged.
20. Reorder. Drag a card by the dotted grip at the left of its title above or below another card. Expected: a bar in the brand orange shows where it will land, the drop zones stay hidden, and after the drop the cards are in the new order. Reload the world; the order is the same. The buttons and the name are not draggable.
21. A reorder is not a move. Drag a card over another card's people. Expected: nobody moves. Drag a person after a reorder; they still move normally.
22. New stages go last. Add a stage by dragging a scene onto the window. Expected: it appears after the cards you have placed.
23. Token row. Expected: every card, including a stage with nobody on it, has the row under it, with the characters whose tokens are on that scene shown normally and the ones that are not dimmed, and the NPCs players own in a second group. Remove that character's token from the scene. Expected: the portrait is dimmed with a dashed edge and nothing drawn over it.
24. Deploy one. Press a dimmed portrait. Expected: you are taken to that scene, there is no formation picker, one click places that one actor's token, and nobody else gets one; the row then shows them present.
24b. Deploy the party. Press the icon at the head of a group (the shield or the paw). Expected: you are taken to that scene and the usual deploy dialog appears with the formation choices and, when player-owned NPCs are missing, the checkbox to include them.
25. Only the party appears. A GM never appears in the row, and an NPC owned only by the GM never does.
26. Reopen. Open the Stage Manager, reload Foundry. Expected: it opens again on its own. Close it with the x, reload. Expected: it stays closed. As a player, nothing opens.
27. Edit. Press the pen on a card. Expected: that scene's configuration sheet opens, whichever scene you are viewing, and nobody's view changes.
28. Live token row. With the panel open, add a token for a party character to a scene, then delete it. Expected: its portrait goes from dimmed to normal and back within a moment, with no reopening. Change an actor's portrait or give a player ownership of an NPC; the row follows.
29. Scene permission icon. Expected: the lower right corner of each card shows a lock for a scene set to GM Only and a people icon for All Players, with a tooltip saying which; change the scene's Permissions in its configuration and the icon follows without reopening the window.
30. Status. Press the bell on a player's chip, three times. Expected: it becomes a mug (Away), then a slashed bell (Do Not Disturb, the chip outlined in the brand orange), then the plain faint bell again; the tooltip names the current status and what the next press sets. Reload the world; the status is still there. The GM's own chip has no bell.
31. Do Not Disturb refuses. Mark a connected player Do Not Disturb and drag them to another card. Expected: they do not move, a notice names them, and no hold pin appears. Mark them Away instead and drag again: they move normally.
32. Do Not Disturb on load. Hold a player on a scene, then mark them Do Not Disturb and have them reload. Expected: they land on the active scene, not the held one.
33. Lobby scene. Press the door icon on a card. Expected: it lights and Lobby Mode becomes available; press it again and Lobby Mode goes back to disabled. Mark a scene nobody is on: it is listed as a stage.
34. Drop a scene. Drag a scene onto the window that already has a card. Expected: nothing is added and a notice says it is already on the panel. Drag a scene from a compendium. Expected: nothing happens. Drag a player chip onto a card. Expected: it still moves the player and nothing is added. Reorder a card by its grip. Expected: it still reorders. The left of the action bar reads "Drag a scene here to add it" and there is no Hold Everyone button.
35. Talk to a stage. Put two players on one scene and a third on another. Press the chat button on the first card. Expected: the Send Toast window opens with exactly those two players ticked and the Entire Party box unticked; send a toast and only those two get it. A card with nobody connected has the button disabled. Mark one of the two Do Not Disturb and press it again: only the other is ticked and a notice names the left-out one; ticking them by hand still works.
36. Chat button on an open Send Toast window. Leave Send Toast open and press the chat button on another card. Expected: the same window comes to the front with that stage's players ticked, no second window.
37. Lobby mode needs a lobby scene. With none marked, Lobby Mode is disabled. Mark one with the door icon: it enables. Turn it on: the button lights and the lobby scene's card gets a Lobby badge. Clear the lobby scene: the mode goes off.
38. Lobby mode on login. With lobby on, have a player log in (or reload) while the active scene is a different one. Expected: within a couple of seconds of the world appearing they land on the lobby scene (the console shows a "Stage manager: load-time move" line with debug on). A player on Do Not Disturb lands on the active scene. A player already connected is not moved by turning it on.
39. Lobby mode off. Turn it off and have the same player reload. Expected: they land on the active scene, or on their own hold if they have one. Nobody connected is moved by turning it off.
40. Lobby with no active scene. With no active scene, turn lobby mode on and have a player log in. Expected: within a few seconds they land on the lobby scene.
41. Lobby scene that is GM only. Set the lobby scene's Permissions to GM Only and turn Lobby Mode on. Expected: a warning says players cannot be sent there. Set it to All Players and a login lands on it.
42. Card titles. A scene with a navigation name shows "Scene Name (Navigation Name)"; one without shows just the name.
