# Stage Manager Architecture

**Audience:** Contributors to the Blacksmith codebase. There is no public API yet; the panel and the setting are the whole surface.

The Stage Manager is a GM Tool window that lists the scenes in use as cards, each holding the people on it as chips. Dragging a chip onto another card sends that player there, and the arrangement is remembered. `scripts/manager-stage.js` is the data and the actions, `scripts/window-stage.js` is the panel, `styles/window-stage.css` holds the little layout that is its own, and the controls are the shared `blacksmith-tool-*` set.

## Three facts from Foundry that shape it

- **Where a person is comes from Foundry.** Every client keeps `user.viewedScene` for every user, kept current by the `userActivity` socket event. The panel reads it and tracks no locations of its own. A person who is not connected is shown on the scene they are held on, if any.
- **A pull is not remembered.** `Scene#pullUsers(users)` makes a player's client view a scene, but a player who reloads lands on the active scene. So what the GM decided is stored and re-applied.
- **The player applies it.** A non-GM client reads the stored assignment on its first `canvasReady` after load and views that scene itself (`StageManager.applyOwnAssignment`, after a short delay so the first draw has settled). It needs no GM connected and cannot race the player's own canvas load, because only that client knows when it is ready. It runs once per load, not on every draw, so a scene the GM pulled a player to with core's own tools is not undone.

## State

One world setting, `stageState`, registered hidden in `settings.js`: `{ assignments: { userId: sceneId }, extras: [sceneId], order: [sceneId] }`. `assignments` is who is held where. `extras` are scenes the GM added to the panel by hand, for a stage nobody is on yet. `order` is the order the GM arranged the cards in. `normalizeState` drops junk and duplicates on every read. World scope so every client can read its own assignment at load; only a GM writes it.

Assigning a player to the active scene clears the hold, since the active scene is where everyone lands anyway.

## Actions

`moveUser` stores the assignment and pulls the player now if they are connected. A GM cannot be held: the GM's own chip only moves the GM's own view (as `viewStage` does), and another GM is left alone. `releaseUser` forgets a hold and leaves the player where they are. `returnEveryone` clears every hold and pulls every connected player to the active scene. `addStage` and `removeStage` edit `extras`. `activateStage` makes a scene the active one (`Scene#activate`), which moves everyone as the scene navigation does. All are GM-only and throw otherwise.

Activating a different scene moves everyone as Foundry does and leaves the holds in place; the held players go back to their held scene the next time they load.

## The panel

`StageManager.stages()` is the view model: the active scene, the scene the GM is viewing, every scene someone is on, every held scene and every hand-added scene, each with its people, whether it is active, how many combats are started on it, and whether it was added by hand. Cards follow the GM's own `order`; a stage not in it goes after the ones that are, the active scene first and then by name, so a world that has never been arranged shows the active scene first.

Each stage is three parts: the title row, the content area, and the drop zone inside the content area. The whole card (`.blacksmith-stage-card`) uses the scene's thumbnail (`scene.thumb`) as its background through a 40% wash of the theme's own `--blacksmith-tool-scrim`: cream on Light, near-black on Dark and Glass, so the picture runs behind the title row and the content area alike. The title row gets a further 30% of the scrim so the name stays readable over a busy part of the map. The content area (`.blacksmith-stage-content`, the card body) has generous padding, which is the visible margin of picture around the drop zone. The drop zone is not drawn at rest: it is only the margin around the people, with no border, wash or padding, so the portraits get the room. While anything is dragged over the window (the panel's own chips or a drag out of Foundry's sidebar) the window gets `is-dragging` and every zone shows a dashed edge and a light wash, going solid orange under the pointer; its border is transparent rather than absent and its height fixed, so nothing shifts when it appears. An empty stage keeps a short row and shows its hint only while dragging. Each person is a solid chip in the theme's raised surface with a 36px round portrait, so a face and a name read over any part of the map. Using the theme's own variables means the text colours stay readable on all three, and the title row, the badges and the buttons are the shared controls with no colours of their own. `scene.thumb` is the small generated thumbnail, not the full map, so it is soft when stretched; the full map would load a large image for every card. The path is rooted with `foundry.utils.getRoute` before it goes into the custom property, because a `url()` inside a custom property resolves against the stylesheet that uses the variable, not the page: a bare world path would otherwise land under this module's `styles` folder and 404. Each stage is a static `blacksmith-tool-card` whose body is a `blacksmith-tool-dropzone`. A chip is a draggable `blacksmith-tool-chip`; players and the GM's own chip are draggable, and another GM's chip is not. Dropping the GM's own chip moves only the GM's own view, exactly what the eye button does. Native HTML drag and drop: `dragover` toggles `is-over`, `drop` resolves who was dropped with `StageManager.usersFromDrop` and calls `moveUser` for each. A drop is understood from the panel's own chips (a user id) and from a drag out of Foundry's sidebar: a User, an Actor, or a Token. An Actor or Token means the players it belongs to: those whose assigned character it is, else those who own it. Another GM is never matched, and a drop that matches nobody warns instead of failing. A held person's chip carries a pin icon and a remove button that releases the hold. A chip shows the player's character portrait, or their user avatar, or an icon when neither exists. A person who is not connected is dashed and dimmed.

Below the people each card has a token row, so the GM can see whether anything needs adding to the scene. It has two groups with a divider: the party's player characters, and the player-owned NPCs (familiars, summons, companions). The row is shown on a stage when a player is on it, so the GM sees what is missing for them, or when any of these already has a token on that scene, so the GM sees who is on the map; a stage with neither stays plain. Each one shows the actor's portrait, never the token's own image, which can be a wildcard path or top-down art that does not read at this size. `StageManager._tokenStatus` marks each one present or missing with `getPartyActorIdsOnCanvas`, the same test the deploy action uses, so what the row shows is what deploying would add. A present token is a round portrait; a missing one is the same portrait dimmed with a dashed edge, nothing drawn over it, and is a button. Pressing it runs `StageManager.deployActor`, which takes the GM to that scene (the placement click needs it on the canvas) and calls Blacksmith's own `deployParty` with the `only` option naming that one actor and the sequential pattern, so there is no formation picker and one click places it. The icon at the head of each group is a button too: it runs `StageManager.deployPartyTo`, the full `deployParty` for the stage with its formation picker and its choice to include the player-owned NPCs. 

A card is reordered by dragging its grip (the dotted handle at the left of the title) above or below another card, with a bar in the brand orange showing where it will land; only the grip drags, so the buttons and the name stay clickable. The drag carries its own type (`application/x-blacksmith-stage`), so the window never mistakes it for moving a person, and the drop zones stay hidden while it is in progress. The new order of every card on screen is saved with `StageManager.setOrder`. Adding a stage is a picker and button in the action bar, on the left, with Return Everyone on the right.

Each card has three buttons: the bullseye makes the scene active (disabled and lit on the one that already is), the eye looks at it yourself (lit on the one you are viewing), and the pen opens the scene's own configuration sheet without moving anyone's view. The first two mirror the scene navigation.

The window re-renders when Foundry does something that changes who is where: `renderSceneNavigation` (which core fires when anyone changes scene), `userConnected`, scene and combat create, update and delete, a token created or deleted and an actor created, updated or deleted (for the token row), and a change to `stageState`. Movement (`updateToken`) is deliberately not watched. All of them are registered through `HookManager`. A redraw is skipped while a chip is being dragged, since it would drop the chip the GM is holding. Hooks are registered through `HookManager` under one context and disposed on close.

## Reopening

The window reopens on load if the GM left it open, through the shared mechanism in `scripts/manager-tool-windows.js`: `StageWindow.open` records `stage` as open and `_onClose` records it closed, in a per-user flag, and `restoreToolWindows` opens it again from `ready`. Only a GM ever records it, and the opener checks that the user is a GM.

## What it does not do

Audio, chat, the world clock and pause are global, whichever scene a player is on. Combat is per scene, but Blacksmith's combat tooling (statistics, the turn and planning timers, the movement-mode switch) still assumes one combat at a time, so a split party running two fights at once is not yet safe. The panel's combat badge only counts started combats on each scene.
